Abstract

Medical laboratory robots must perform contact-rich manipulation while preserving the action order prescribed by operating protocols. End-to-end imitation-learning and vision--language--action policies may confuse visually similar workflow stages, producing stage-inconsistent actions over a long horizon. We present Robo-MLT, a protocol-grounded dual-system framework that separates explicit workflow monitoring from continuous action generation. A frozen vision--language planner reads an initial observation and instantiates an ordered sequence over an atomic-skill library. A lightweight execution monitor then tracks progress with two heads: a completion head advances a plan pointer once the active skill has completed, while a back head recognizes when human intervention has left the workspace in a state from which that skill can no longer be carried out, and triggers bounded, protocol-constrained replanning. Conditioned on the active skill, a Qwen3-VL-based flow-matching executor generates action chunks that an asynchronous runtime streams to the robot at the control-loop rate. Across 20 paired configurations on blood gas analysis and blood sample tube sorting, Robo-MLT carries more samples through their full workflow than four learning-based baselines, improves end-to-end task success rate by 15\% over the strongest hierarchical baseline π0.5 on both tasks, and recovers from mid-procedure interruptions that leave a fixed-program pipeline stuck.

Method Overview

Robo-MLT couples System 2, a low-frequency semantic reasoner that tracks the active atomic skill, with System 1, a high-frequency generative executor that maps that skill to continuous actions. A zero-shot Qwen3.7-Max planner reads the task instruction, instrument protocol, and initial workspace observation to compose an ordered sequence over a declarative skill library — seven skills for blood gas analysis and five for tube sorting. The plan is instantiated directly from the observed scene without gradient updates.

System 2 uses a multi-frame dual-head execution monitor built on a frozen SigLIP2 image encoder and temporal Transformer. A completion head advances the plan when the active skill finishes, while a back head detects intervention-induced states from which that skill can no longer be completed. After temporal debouncing, the monitor returns STAY, ADVANCE, or RECOVER. Recovery invokes a replanner constrained by a call budget and hard protocol rules; a per-skill stall timeout can trigger the same path.

System 1 executes the active skill with a Qwen3-VL-conditioned controller and flow-matching action head that generates 30-step chunks. An asynchronous runtime lets System 2 update at 1.33 Hz while System 1 prefetches and blends chunks to maintain the nominal 20 Hz control loop.

Overview of Robo-MLT. A Qwen3.7-Max planner creates an ordered atomic-skill sequence. System 2 monitors progress and triggers bounded, protocol-constrained replanning when needed, while System 1 executes the active skill with a Qwen3-VL-conditioned flow-matching policy.

Contributions

  1. We propose Robo-MLT, a hierarchical framework separating low-frequency workflow monitoring from high-frequency generative control for long-horizon laboratory manipulation.
  2. We develop a lightweight multi-frame execution monitor that detects skill completion and when human intervention should trigger replanning.
  3. On two real-world medical laboratory tasks, Robo-MLT reaches end-to-end success rates of 85% and 75% and sample completion rates of 92.50% and 94.38%, improving average success by 15 points over π0.5.

Experiment Demonstrations

Plan progression and skill switching during rollout

Each clip shows synchronized camera views alongside execution-monitor predictions, plan-pointer switches, and ground-truth skill boundaries. The overlay summarizes the current plan step, active skill, and System 2 action.

Blood gas analysis. The robot executes a long-horizon blood gas analysis plan. When the operator removes the in-hand sample, the system detects the disruption, triggers recovery, replans, and completes the task with the revised plan.

Blood sample tube sorting. The robot completes a tube-sorting task without intervention. Skill completion drives each plan transition, progressing sequentially through all steps until the robot returns to its home position.

Target movement during an isolated grasp skill

Two disturbance tests isolate different capabilities: moving a target during an isolated grasp tube from rack skill tests closed-loop re-targeting, while removing the in-hand sample during the blood gas workflow tests RECOVER and System 2 replanning.

Fixed-Program. A single-skill baseline that localizes the tube once, selects a pre-calibrated waypoint, and executes open-loop approach–grasp–lift. It succeeds in 20/20 static trials per tube category and 0/20 disturbed trials.

Robo-MLT (ours). The closed-loop executor re-targets and grasps the moved tube. Both π0 and Robo-MLT succeed in 20/20 static and 20/20 disturbed trials per tube category; this comparison isolates closed-loop re-targeting rather than an advantage of System 2.

Stage-confusion error under perceptual aliasing

Representative perceptual-aliasing failure of end-to-end IL and VLA baselines. The scenes before withdrawal and after insertion are nearly identical. Without explicit workflow progress, the policy re-inserts the tube after withdrawal and cycles between stages instead of advancing to disposal.
