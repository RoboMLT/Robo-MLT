Abstract

Medical laboratory robots must perform contact-rich manipulation while preserving the action order prescribed by operating protocols. End-to-end imitation-learning and vision–language–action policies may confuse visually similar workflow stages, producing stage-inconsistent actions over a long horizon. We present Robo-MLT, a protocol-grounded dual-system framework that separates explicit workflow monitoring from continuous action generation. A frozen vision–language planner reads an initial observation and instantiates an ordered sequence over an atomic-skill library. A lightweight execution monitor then tracks progress with two heads: a completion head advances a plan pointer once the active skill has completed, while a back head recognizes when human intervention has left the workspace in a state from which that skill can no longer be carried out, and triggers bounded, protocol-constrained replanning. Conditioned on the active skill, a Qwen3-VL-based flow-matching executor generates action chunks that an asynchronous runtime streams to the robot at the control-loop rate. Across 20 paired configurations on blood gas analysis and blood sample tube sorting, Robo-MLT carries more samples through their full workflow than four learning-based baselines, improves end-to-end task success by 15 percentage points over the strongest hierarchical baseline π0.5 on both tasks, and recovers from mid-procedure interruptions that leave a fixed-program pipeline stuck.

Method Overview

Robo-MLT comprises two coupled components: System 2, a low-frequency semantic reasoner that monitors execution and maintains the active atomic skill, and System 1, a high-frequency generative executor that maps the active skill to continuous actions. A frozen vision–language planner initializes an ordered sequence over a declarative atomic-skill library. System 2 tracks this plan with a multi-frame, dual-head execution monitor. A completion head advances the plan pointer once the active skill has completed, while a back head recognizes when human intervention has left the workspace in a state from which that skill can no longer be carried out and triggers bounded, protocol-constrained replanning. System 1 executes the active skill with a Qwen3-VL-conditioned controller and a flow-matching action head. An asynchronous runtime coordinates the two systems: System 2 updates at 0.22 Hz while System 1 streams actions at the 20 Hz control-loop rate.

Contributions

  1. We propose Robo-MLT, a hierarchical framework that separates explicit, low-frequency workflow monitoring from high-frequency generative control for long-horizon, fine-grained medical laboratory manipulation.
  2. We develop a lightweight, multi-frame execution monitor that maintains an explicit representation of workflow progress, with two decision heads that determine when the active skill has completed and when human intervention should trigger replanning of the remaining plan.
  3. We evaluate Robo-MLT on two real-world medical laboratory tasks against IL, VLA, and conventional fixed-program baselines, reporting task success, per-sample completion, disturbance response, and runtime cost.

Experiment Demonstrations

Reacting to a mid-task disturbance

Fixed-program pipeline. Senses the tube once, then moves open-loop; when it is shifted mid-motion, the arm closes on the empty slot.

Robo-MLT (ours). Closed-loop control re-targets to the tube's new position and completes the grasp.

Repeated docking under perceptual aliasing

End-to-end IL/VLA. Insertion and withdrawal look alike at the inlet, so the policy re-issues the docking action instead of withdrawing, and the procedure stalls.
